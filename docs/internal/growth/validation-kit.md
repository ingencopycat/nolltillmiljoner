# B52 — Founder validation kit

Run manually; no outreach has been sent. Recruit 8–12 Swedish-speaking private
investors who seriously evaluated a stock in the last 90 days, including people
who decided not to buy. Aim for 4 with active written theses, 4 who use scattered
notes/spreadsheets and 2–4 who abandoned a research workflow. Include nonfollowers
and Nordic-focused investors, not only enthusiastic friends. Do not require
Discord, a portfolio size, brokerage access or financial screenshots.

## Recruitment and consent

Founder can invite volunteers through existing channels after reviewing this
copy: “Jag undersöker hur privata investerare följer upp sina egna antaganden.
Har du nyligen utvärderat en aktie? Jag söker ett 30-minuters samtal om hur du
arbetade. Du behöver inte dela innehav, belopp eller privata dokument. NTM är
frivilligt att prova och samtalet innehåller ingen investeringsrådgivning.”

Explain purpose, voluntary participation and how to withdraw notes. Ask consent
to take notes; recording is off by default and requires separate explicit consent.
Use participant codes. Keep contact-to-code mapping separately on an access-controlled
device. Proposed retention: delete raw notes/contact mapping within 90 days of
synthesis, sooner on withdrawal; retain only de-identified themes if consent
covers this. Confirm the policy with the owner before recruitment. No private
notes, actual thesis text, holdings or identities belong in this public repository.

## Interview guide (30 minutes)

1. 0–3 min: explain purpose and consent. “Tell me about the last stock you seriously considered.”
2. 3–12 min: “When was that? What did you actually do first? Where did you keep your notes? What sources did you use? What made you stop or continue?” Invite a description, not sensitive documents.
3. 12–20 min: “What happened when the next report arrived? Did you revisit your original assumptions? How did you find them? What was annoying? What did you forget? What consequence did that have?” If no later report arrived, record no opportunity rather than no retention.
4. 20–25 min: “What did you try to fix this? What do you currently pay for? What was your last research/tool subscription purchase or cancellation, and why? Which task justified that expense? What did you stop paying for?” Amounts are optional and recorded only with consent.
5. 25–30 min: only now show the smallest relevant existing NTM workflow. Ask them to perform a task using invented/non-sensitive example text. Observe unaided behavior, time and confusion. “What would you do next?” Do not explain every control or ask “Would you use an AI investing app?”

Log prompts/help separately from unaided actions. Compliments, feature requests,
waitlist entries and hypothetical willingness to pay are weak signals. Never
equate market returns or thesis correctness with product success.

## Behavioral pilot definitions

Activation: on their own device a participant saves a usable thesis/plan with at
least one explicit assumption and a review date or report question, then can
reopen it in Min NTM without founder help. Record date and help level with consent,
not the thesis text. Saving alone is not retention.

Second-review retention: at a later genuine report or self-selected review date,
the participant independently returns, opens the original assumptions and records
an explicit keep/revise/close/abstain review. An interview-driven revisit is
“prompted,” never counted as independent retention. Record first eligible review
opportunity, first review, second eligible opportunity and second review separately.
For the stronger two-cycle signal require a second distinct review after the first;
the first save is not a review. Exclude “no opportunity yet” from the eligible
denominator and report exclusions. Allow 6–10 weeks or a complete report cycle;
do not declare failure simply because reports occur quarterly.

## Synthesis and decision rubric

Copy: cohort/time window; recruited/interviewed/activated/eligible/returned counts;
prompted vs independent visits; common task; evidence codes; frequency/severity;
current workaround/cost; strongest contradiction; missing segment; one proposed
change; owner; next test date; build/iterate/defer/stop decision and reason.

Proposed thresholds, to agree before observing results: proceed with one small
workflow experiment if at least 5 of 8 interviews describe the same recurring
problem and an actual workaround, at least 6 of 10 pilots activate, and at least
4 of 6 eligible pilots complete two independent reviews. Always report n/N;
small samples guide learning and are not population estimates. If denominators
are not reached, extend recruitment/opportunities rather than call it success.

Iterate when the problem repeats but unaided activation fails. Defer monetization
when use returns only after founder prompts or no existing time/money tradeoff
exists. Stop the proposed feature when repeated interviews find no recurring
problem, users are satisfied with their workaround or the feature is sought only
for recommendations. Red flags: polite praise, friends-only sample, giveaway
traffic, clicks with no saved work, requests for generic AI, no second visit,
cherry-picked quotes and unrecorded assistance. Log negative evidence first.

Use [the blank pilot tracker](pilot-tracker.template.json) privately. Existing
analytics are memory-only/coarse and cannot establish longitudinal user retention;
do not introduce persistent IDs or upload private local storage to fill this gap.
